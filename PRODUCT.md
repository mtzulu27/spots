# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Spots is for people around 18 to 35 in Cali with active or semi-active social lives: students, young professionals, couples, friend groups, and people who want to go out but do not always know where to go. They open the app when they need to resolve a plan for today, find a place for a date, discover something new to eat or drink, go out for an afternoon, celebrate, work remotely, or save ideas for later.

## Product Purpose

Spots helps people discover and decide on plans, places, and experiences in the city quickly, visually, and with curation. It turns the question "what should we do?" into concrete options based on mood, moment, budget, company, and location.

The core problem is decision friction. Users are not only missing information about places; they are stuck between too many scattered options, repeated recommendations, incomplete details, and social pressure to choose well. Spots should prevent choosing a plan from becoming a mini research project across Instagram, Google Maps, TikTok, and group chats.

The primary job is: help me quickly decide on a good place or plan for this moment. Secondary jobs include saving places for later, comparing options by mood and context, resolving a plan with friends, finding something that feels new without feeling risky, and opening enough detail to act.

## Brand Personality

Local, curated, fresh, close, and spontaneous.

Spots should feel like deciding with someone who knows the city, not like filtering a database. It should leave users with a sense of "now I know what to do", "this feels chosen for me", and "Cali has more going on than I thought."

The experience should be local but refined, without cliches. Cali should come through through places, neighborhoods, moments of use, categories, and occasional language cues, not through forced folklore or cultural caricature.

## Anti-references

Spots should not feel like a generic directory, a Google Maps clone, a cold reservations app, an institutional event listing, a saturated marketplace, or an endless grid with no point of view.

Avoid visuals and interaction patterns that feel overly corporate, overly tech/SaaS, tourist-cliche, overloaded with filters, or like a generic app template. The product should not prioritize only what is nearest or most popular, because that would make the experience obvious and interchangeable.

## Design Principles

Decide before browsing forever.

Curation over quantity.

Visual first, with useful details close at hand.

Every place should suggest an occasion, not only a category.

Reduce the anxiety of choosing.

Locality with judgment, not cliches.

The feed should feel alive, not saturated.

The detail view should answer: why go, with whom, when, how much, and where.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Spots should aim for WCAG AA contrast and legibility. Typography should be clear, touch targets should be comfortable, motion should respect reduced-motion preferences, states should not depend on color alone, text over images must remain readable, and language should be inclusive.

Animations may feel alive, but they should never block the task or slow down decision-making.
